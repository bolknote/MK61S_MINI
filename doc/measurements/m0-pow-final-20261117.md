# Final RP2040 pow rounding decision

The RP2040 rescue keeps its 512-bit, 1,024-bit, exact small integer and
4,096-bit stages. If those stages do not settle rounding, it compares the
same midpoint at 8,192 bits. An overlap there calls an exact comparator
for the two dyadic powers. If the interval code has no adjacent-pair hint,
the comparator searches the ordered positive encodings in at most 63
steps. The rescue restores the result sign after rounding the magnitude.
The final undecided trap has been removed from the RP2040 path.

## Exactness and memory

Write the positive base as `A*2^e`, the midpoint as `M*2^f` and
the reduced absolute exponent as `p/q`. For positive exponents the
comparison is between `A^p*2^(e*p)` and `M^q*2^(f*q)`. For negative
exponents it compares their product with one and reverses the side.
The exponents of two are aligned by removing the smaller scale from
both sides. The most significant differing word gives the order.
Exact equality selects the endpoint with the even encoding. This
includes the midpoint at half the smallest subnormal and the overflow
boundary.

Each radix-65,536 digit is the low word of an exact convolution diagonal
plus the carry from the preceding diagonal. The high words are the carry
into the next column. Squaring evaluates one half of each diagonal and
counts off-diagonal terms twice. The two arrays retain small integer
powers in place. The old operand is moved to the high end before
writing the product from low to high, so every overwritten input word
has already made its final contribution. A missing child digit is
recomputed with the same carry recurrence. The explicit stack holds the
parent state while visiting a child. A cache hit changes only the work.

The existing exponent guards give `p < 2^63` and `q <= 2^116`.
When `q > 1`, `p < 2^53`. Accordingly the expression has at most
223 nodes, including literals and the possible reciprocal product.
The longest dependency path contains at most 125 multiplications.
Coefficients use fewer than `55*2^116` bits. The word count is below
`2^118`, so adding the diagonal and its incoming carry takes fewer
than 150 bits. The retained arrays need at most 43 accumulator bits.
Aligned bit positions fit in signed 128 bits, since their magnitudes
are below `(1075+54)*(p+q)+1`. These bounds apply to the decoded finite
positive domain after the existing saturation and tiny-exponent rules.

Each root scan and diagonal has a finite index range. Each child visit
descends to an earlier node. The exact fallback therefore terminates
without a fixed-precision separation hypothesis. Its fixed allocations
are on the stack, and the compiler's deepest arithmetic call chain is
below 26 KiB on the 32 KiB Pico H foreground stack. There is no heap
allocation or extra static RAM. The long loops call PicoCalc's existing
watchdog service at bounded intervals while doing work. The exact
fallback has no practical universal latency bound. In the worst
theoretical coefficient range, recomputing discarded digits can take
far longer than a calculator operation.

## Qualification

The host test checked 1,383 midpoint comparisons against full Python
integer powers. A smaller retained buffer forced recomputation on a
further 111 comparisons. The linked Pico H ELF passed ten forced ARM
cases with factory BootROM, including both signs, an exact midpoint,
underflow, overflow, a streamed integer product, and the no-hint
search. The longest 8,192-bit workload made 5,408 calls to the production
watchdog hook. ARM emulation verifies scheduling and numerical results.
The silicon watchdog requires a separate physical measurement.
